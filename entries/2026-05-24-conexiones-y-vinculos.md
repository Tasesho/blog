# Relationships and Bonds

What are the **bonds** and **relationships** we create, really?

That is one of the main themes behind games like **Persona 3 Reload** and **Death Stranding**. I recently finished both of them, and I wanted to share some reflections they left me with.

### Why Do We Need Bonds?

In-game, we build **Social Links** or expand the **Chiral Network** by connecting different nodes. On the gameplay side, this gives us benefits and progression, but more importantly, it gives meaning to our mission.

In both games, there is a mission that must be completed no matter what.

In **Persona 3 Reload**, building bonds becomes essential because, when the final judgment day arrives, those relationships are what give you the strength and support needed to face the end.

In **Death Stranding**, the goal is to reconnect the entire continent, rebuild **human connections**, and uncover the mysteries behind the Death Stranding itself.

But the real beauty behind these bonds is the message they try to give to you, the player.

One of the **Social Links** I remember the most from **Persona 3 Reload** is the **Sun Arcana**, **Akinari Kamiki**. His story as someone living with a terminal illness, fully aware that one day he will die, is devastating.

While playing, you slowly realize the message hidden behind his story:
live in the **present**.

**Death Stranding** carries a very similar feeling. Throughout the game, we are constantly reminded that the BTs are people who died and became trapped between worlds.

Both games constantly revolve around death, but instead of becoming hopeless, they try to tell you something else:
***carpe diem***.

Enjoy the present while it still exists.

And I think that's what fascinates me the most.

How can a medium like **video games** touch such painful topics while still feeling incredibly hopeful?

Both games tell you that, no matter what happens in life, you still have to keep moving forward.

Sam Porter goes through endless tragedies during his journey to reconnect the continent, yet he still continues.

Makoto loses friends and faces unbearable situations, yet he still chooses to keep going in order to save the world.

All of this reminded me of something I once read in Emotional Intelligence by Daniel Goleman. There is a section where he talks about how traumatic experiences can change people forever, and how the brain sometimes hides or suppresses painful memories almost as a survival mechanism.

It's as if the mind already knows that certain moments will permanently change who we are.

### Returning to Human Connections

Going back to the idea of bonds, do we really need them?

Personally, I think we do.

At the end of the day, coming back home after studying or working and finding nobody there, only silence and emptiness, would slowly destroy almost anyone emotionally.

Relationships are more than **important**.
They are almost a **human necessity**.

And honestly, this goes beyond fiction for me.

During my time at university, I met a lot of people, but despite that, I still felt lonely.

I managed to make a few friends, but I couldn't keep strengthening those relationships over time. It felt like being trapped inside a cell where I couldn't scream or even speak properly, only watch time continue moving forward.

Eventually, my social circle slowly became smaller and smaller because I rarely spent time with the people I met.

And if you're wondering why I didn't simply talk to more people:
I was shy, and most of the time it felt easier to do nothing.

I hated that feeling, but at the same time, I also wasn't doing much to change it.

I simply let time pass.

After finishing the modern Persona trilogy (3, 4, and 5), I realized I needed to change something.

I couldn't keep allowing time to move forward while doing nothing.

But as contradictory as it sounds, I still left things exactly the same.

Time passed, and eventually I left university.

And with that, most of the relationships I had built disappeared too.

### Strengthening Bonds

Despite all of that, there was one thing I did decide to change:
strengthening the relationships I still had.

I think there are very few moments in life where you can truly say:

> "I changed for the better."

For a long time, I felt like my opinions and ideals changed constantly, but none of it ever felt meaningful. Most of the time, I simply felt indifferent, like I was just another gear inside a much bigger machine.

But now, for the first time, I can genuinely say this with confidence:

**I became a **better person**.**

And honestly, I feel proud of that.

Without even fully realizing it, simply by following what felt right to me, I slowly became more present in the lives of my close friends and family. Certain experiences can completely change the way you see life.

So how exactly did I change?

Honestly?

I don't really know.

There is no universal guide explaining how to react to every situation in life. Every person responds differently to the exact same experience.

But I guess that's part of being human.

Sometimes we fail.
Sometimes we succeed.
And through both, we slowly learn who we are.

A few years ago, if you had placed me in front of someone having a panic attack or an anxiety crisis, I genuinely wouldn't have known what to do.

Now, I understand that every person needs support in different ways.

Some people simply need someone to stay beside them in silence.
Others need reassurance, conversation, physical presence, or someone grounding them back into reality.

Experience is what teaches those things.

And like Daniel Goleman mentioned in his book, shocking experiences are often the ones that remain in our minds the longest. They can change us permanently, for better or for worse.

### Your Persona

The last topic I want to touch on — and I'll keep it short — is the idea of the **persona**.

**Carl Jung** described the **persona** as the version of ourselves shaped by ideals, beliefs, and the image we present to the world.

Its opposite is the **shadow**:
the part made from repressed emotions, fears, insecurities, and the things we hide beneath the surface so others don't see them.

I think it's important to recognize both.

To understand what belongs to your real self and what belongs to the parts you try to suppress.

And even if you still don't fully accept those parts of yourself, I think that's okay too.

One of the biggest strengths we have as humans is the ability to change.

If I sat down and talked with the version of myself from two or three years ago, I honestly don't think he would recognize me anymore.

Too many things happened.
Too many experiences changed me.

And because of that, I think it's incredibly important to understand who you are, what you like, what you dislike, and what kind of person you want to become.

Your experiences shape you.

Almost every thought, fear, conviction, or dream we carry comes from something we lived through.

## Wrapping Up

When I started writing this, I thought it would simply become a small reflection about two games I recently finished.

But somewhere along the way, it turned into something much more personal.

I think games like Persona 3 Reload and Death Stranding resonate with so many people because, beneath all the gameplay and fantasy elements, they talk about something deeply human:
connection.

The fear of **loneliness**.
The **pain** of loss.
The **desire** to be understood.
The need to keep moving forward even after life hurts you.

At the end of the day, relationships are probably one of the few things that truly give meaning to our experiences.

People leave marks on us.
Some stay for years.
Others disappear almost instantly.
But every connection changes us in one way or another.

And maybe that's why life feels so strange sometimes.

We are constantly becoming new versions of ourselves through the people we meet, the things we lose, and the experiences we survive.

So if there is one thing these games reminded me of, it is this:

Enjoy the **present**.
Appreciate the people around you.
Say the things you want to say while you still can.

Because time keeps moving forward whether we notice it or not.

*Carpe diem.*
