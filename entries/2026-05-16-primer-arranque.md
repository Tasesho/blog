# Who Am I

Hello **internet**.

I'm **Tase**: a computer science student, self-taught developer, **Linux** enthusiast, and someone who spends way too much time customizing my PC and rebuilding perfectly functional systems for absolutely no reason.

Most of the things I know about **technology** didn't come from **lessons**.

They came from **curiosity**.

From breaking **Windows** installations while trying to optimize them.
From doing **technical support** for family members.
From searching **random forums** at 3 AM trying to understand why something stopped working.
From opening the **browser inspector** on websites just to figure out how they were built.

**This blog** exists mostly because I wanted a place where I could document all of that.

Not only **projects** or **technical things**, but also the **process** behind learning them.

## Why This Blog Exists

The idea of creating a blog started after one of my university teachers mentioned how useful it was to document projects and experiences over time.

That thought stayed in my head for months.

At first, I looked into platforms like WordPress or Blogger, but none of them really felt right to me. I wanted something simpler, more personal, and fully under my control.

So eventually I ended up building this blog myself.

Not because it was the easiest option.
Probably the opposite, honestly.

But there is something special about having your own small place on the internet.

No algorithms.
No feeds.
No optimization.
Just a collection of thoughts, projects, mistakes, experiments, and random things I find interesting.

## Things I Like

Most of my interests are connected in one way or another to **technology**.

I enjoy **Linux**, **self-hosting**, **scripting**, **terminal workflows**, **minimal systems**, and learning how things work underneath the surface instead of simply using them.

Outside of tech, I also spend a lot of time watching **anime**, playing **games**, listening to **music**, and occasionally cooking when I need a break from staring at a terminal for six hours straight.

I also really like old **internet culture**.

**Personal websites**.
**Forums**.
**Small blogs**.
People building things just because they wanted to.

I think part of this blog comes from missing that version of the internet a little.
